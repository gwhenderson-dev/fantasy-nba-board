Back-to-back sitters

Detected from the game logs (last season + this one): a second night counts as a rest sit only when the player played the night before and his team’s next game, so injury spells don’t count, whatever the injury tag said. Players who sat a lot of second nights last season kept doing it the next season, just less often (correlation 0.25), so each rate is pulled toward the league’s 11%, and this season’s second nights count four times as much as last season’s. Add anyone you know won’t play second nights below (one name per line); they count as certain to sit.

How it works

Everything on this site comes from one model built on NBA box scores. No outside projections are mixed in: ESPN and FantasyPros appear only as benchmarks. This page writes out every formula the site uses, region by region, with the numbers in it and the test behind each one. Every piece was tested on past seasons before it shipped, and the ideas that failed stay in the record.

1. Scoring: one stat line, any league

The site never stores fantasy points. Every projection, every game and every luck number is a stat line (points, makes and attempts, threes, rebounds, offensive rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, turnovers) plus the chance of a double-, triple- or quadruple-double. Your browser turns a line into fantasy points with your league’s rules:

FP = Σ stat × your points for that stat + P(double-double) × DD bonus + P(triple-double) × TD bonus + P(quad-double) × QD bonus

If your league’s bonuses don’t stack, a triple-double earns only the TD bonus, so the formula uses P(DD) − P(TD) for the double-double term. Change the rules on the League tab and every number on the site re-scores.

2. The draft-day projection

Built once before the season and frozen at tip-off (the Projections tab’s “Preseason” view). Each step below changes the stat line; the order is the order the model applies them.

Production per minute

rate(stat) = Σ w_y × stat_y / Σ w_y × minutes_y w = 7, 2, 1 (last season, the one before, the one before that)

Minutes-weighted, so a season of cameo minutes barely counts. 7/2/1 beat the classic 5/4/3 in 11 of 12 test seasons; four- and five-season memories and two-season memories both did worse.

Age

under 27: rate × (1 + 0.016 × (27 − age)) over 27: rate × (1 − 0.024 × (age − 27)) over 27 and age-free FP/G ≥ 35: rate × (1 − 0.012 × (age − 27))

The gentler slope for players good enough to keep their role (old stars hold up, old role players don’t) was checked three ways: a placebo test, a bootstrap over seasons, and the error on exactly the players it touches (3.95 vs 4.80 FP/G without it). Removing the age curve made drafts significantly worse.

Minutes and games

MPG = (Σ w × minutes + 5 × league MPG) / (Σ w × games + 5) then MPG = 0.7 × MPG + 0.3 × his MPG over his last 20 games availability = (Σ w × games + 2 × league rate) / (Σ w × seasons on a roster + 2)

The late-season blend (only for players with 20+ games who appeared in their team’s last 15) cut the minutes error in 11 of 11 test seasons. The 2 in the availability formula is deliberately small so durability differences show.

Team context and form

crowding: FP/G − 7.09 × (team load − league mean load) load = Σ (usage% × MPG × availability) / 4800 preseason: FP/G + min(0.13, 0.00136 × preseason min) × (preseason FP/36 − projected FP/36) impact: FP/G + 0.258 × (last season plus-minus per 36 − 0.20)

Crowding corrects teams whose players were each projected in roles they held on other teams (fitted, not imposed). Preseason form cut the error in 11 of 12 seasons, and impact (plus-minus) in 11 of 12; preseason is capped at ±25% and impact at ×0.6 to ×1.4 of the line.

Availability news at the draft

out on opening night: games = (82 − lost) × projected games / 82 lost = the team's announced timeline, else 29.6 (surgery, Achilles, ACL, fracture) or 12.2 (other) ended last season hurt: games − 4.84 returned from a lost season: production × 0.967 / 0.927 / 0.883 (pre-injury FP/G 40+ / 30–40 / under 30)

Rookies

FP/36 from draft slot + college production (+ centre term) MPG from draft slot + minutes open on his new team games from draft slot then FP/36 moves toward his preseason FP/36 by 0.21 (under 80 min) or 0.36

The college model beat ESPN’s rookie lines in 7 of 7 test seasons on the realistic objective.

3. Rest of season: how far a few games move him

After every game the draft-day line is blended with the season so far. Each piece of the draft-day line counts as a fixed amount of evidence, tuned on 1999–2019 and scored once on 2021–26:

rate(stat) = (draft rate × 600 + season total) / (600 + season minutes) MPG = (5 × draft MPG + season minutes) / (5 + games) availability = (20 × draft availability + games played) / (20 + team games so far) bonuses: same as rates, evidence = 600 / draft MPG games

This blend beat both “never update” and “trust the season so far” at all 25 test checkpoints (two weeks in to late January). A later check (Nov 2026) re-tuned the weights for the last third of the season (days 112–140): the best setting was the same 600 minutes / 5 games / 20 games, and dropping the preseason projection late in the season was clearly worse (+0.12 FP/G error, 4 of 5 test seasons worse). Its result is the ROS FP/G used everywhere in-season.

Rookies get their own setting (Nov 2026). The weights above were tuned on veterans, whose draft-day line rests on years of NBA games; a rookie’s rests on a college translation, which is far less reliable. Tested the same way on 502 rookie seasons (each rookie’s college-model line built only from earlier draft classes; tuned 2012–19, scored 2021–26, checked after his own game 5, 10, 15, 20 and 30), the best rule trusts his real games faster when he is beating the projection:

rate(stat) = (draft rate × k + season total) / (k + season minutes) k = 100 min if he is above his draft FP per minute, else 400 MPG = (g × draft MPG + minutes in his last 5 games) / (g + 5) g = 2 if his last-5 MPG is above his draft MPG, else 10 availability = (10 × draft availability + games played) / (10 + team games so far)

Rest-of-season error: 5.85 FP/G with the veteran setting, 5.31 with one rookie setting for everyone, 4.78 with the faster-when-beating-it rule (better in 22 of 25 test checkpoints). Rookies outplaying their projection by 20%+ had been under-projected by about 3 FP/G. What did not work: dropping the draft-day line entirely after 5, 8, 10, 15, 20 or 30 games (every version worse — a handful of games is noisy, and rookies who start slowly often recover), separate settings by position, and counting games played while a key teammate sat for less (rookies do play about 2 more minutes in those games, but the extra minutes largely stick). The ROS FP/G feeds:

ROS points = ROS FP/G × availability × games left + injury boost (section 5) games left = his team's remaining games (for a player who is out: from his return date)

4. Luck

Every game’s surprise (actual minus expected) splits exactly into pieces. Each piece was tested for whether it predicts the next game, over 154,305 player-games (2016–26, comparing each player with himself):

  • Carries over: extra minutes (a quarter of a minutes spike persists, 8/8 seasons), extra shot attempts (8/8), extra assists (8/8; passing creates them).
  • Luck: 2-point % (zero persistence), 3-point % (slightly anti-persistent), steals, blocks, rebounds, free-throw %, bonuses. A turnover spike reverses.
luck-adjusted line, per game: every stat = his draft-day rate × minutes played extra attempts A = actual FGA − expected FGA FGA = actual; FGM += his FG% × A 3PM += his threes per FGA × A PTS += (2 × FG% + 3PM per FGA) × A AST = actual bonus chances × minutes / draft MPG luck = actual FP − FP(luck-adjusted line)

“His” rates are the draft-day projection, so a five-steal night from a one-steal player is mostly luck. Season luck per game = average luck over his games. Feeding luck-adjusted numbers into the rest-of-season blend was tested and changed nothing: the blend’s slow trust already discounts luck.

5. Injuries and injury boosts

Who is out

ESPN’s fantasy feed lags the league’s report, so the official NBA injury report (published every 15 minutes on game days, pulled hourly) comes first:

his status = his row on the latest report that covers his team (his team is on it but he isn't = cleared) streak = how many of his team's reports in a row list him Out Out, streak 1 = out for that game only (rest, a knock) Out, streak 2+ = out until a report clears him otherwise ESPN's status (OUT / IR / SUSP / DTD)

Health tags are solid: OUT (also IR, SUSP) DOUBT DTD Q PROB. Everything else is an outline: B2B sits back-to-backs, WVR on waivers, RK rookie, ▲ +2.5 FP · Edwards out injury boost (extra FP per game, naming who is out), DNP healthy but out of the rotation.

When the two sources disagree: if the official report covers his team’s next game and doesn’t list him, he counts as available even when ESPN’s fantasy feed still says OUT (ESPN lags the report both ways). A healthy player with no minutes in any of his team’s last 5 games is treated as out of the rotation: his games don’t count on Waivers or My week, and he can’t inherit an injured teammate’s minutes, until he plays (or was on the injury report in the last few days). When the official report lists a player, it decides: Out on two reports in a row (or Out once with an ESPN return date after that game) is a multi-game absence; Out once with no later date is that game only. Rest and personal days mean back next game. A name on the report we can’t match never clears anyone on that team with the same surname.

Doubtful, questionable, probable (src/backtest/status_play.py, official game-day reports 2023-26): players listed doubtful played 1% of the time (784 listings), questionable 50% (41% when coming back from a layoff; 5,082), probable 93% (83%; 1,882). That game counts at that chance on Waivers and My week (a doubtful game is treated as not played unless you tick it). Back from a layoff (5+ team games missed): his first game back averages 83% of his usual minutes and the second 92%, then normal (1,527 returns, 2019-26), so those two games are scaled down.

How long he is out

return date = your weeks-out (Injury boosts panel), else ESPN's expected return date, else the historical mean absence for that body part minus games already missed

Who gains his minutes

For a key absentee (out, played in his team’s last 5, 24+ minutes a game), tested on 7,669 past absences with every parameter the same in all 10 leave-one-season-out folds:

weight_i = (1 + same position group) × √max(1, 38 − his MPG) +min_i = 0.6 × absentee's minutes × weight_i / Σ weights lone backup centre: at least +4.8 min boost FP/G = +min × his own FP per minute (only on dates before the return)

The top predicted beneficiary gained +3.3 minutes against +3.7 predicted. Starters near their minutes cap gain little; bench players with room gain most. Boosts feed every rest-of-season number: Waivers, Drops, Sell high, My week, trades, Projections.

Usage: per-minute production also rises

When the absentee was a high-usage player, teammates also produce more per minute, most at his position. Tested on the same absences (63,096 teammate-games): top-usage-third absentees lifted teammates by +0.46 FP per 30 minutes, same position +0.62, positive in 9 of 10 seasons, and adding it improved game predictions in all 10. It is small and missed the bar set before the test, so it was added on the owner’s call.

absentee usage uK = (FGA + 0.44 × FTA + TOV) per minute usage bump (FP/min) = −0.0012 + 0.0419 × (uK / 0.53 − 1) + 0.0050 × same position group (never below 0) boost FP/G = +min × his FP per minute + usage bump × (his minutes + extra minutes)

7. In-season tab

buy-low gap = luck-adjusted FP/G − actual FP/G listed if: other team, 3+ games, gap > 0, max(actual, luck-adjusted) ≥ 20 sell high = actual FP/G − luck-adjusted FP/G (your players) now vs draft = actual FP/G − draft-day FP/G

Early in the season managers judge players against what they drafted them to be; the best buy-lows pair a big gap with a disappointing start. “Ran cold on” lists the two luck pieces behind the gap, per game.

Drops: gain = best free agent's ROS points − your player's ROS points (free agent must share an ESPN position) drop candidate if gain > 8% of your player's ROS points never: keep list, IR slot

Rosters ranks teams by Σ ROS points; “luck so far” = Σ (actual − luck-adjusted) points of the current roster.

8. Waivers tab

per game = play × (ROS FP/G + matchup + boost) Projected = Σ per game over the window

Matchup, from 224,300 player-games over 16 seasons (each factor is the opponent’s season-to-date value divided by the league’s):

matchup = 22.0 × (opponent pace − 1) + 6.4 × (FP the opponent allows to his position − 1) + 16.6 × (FG% the opponent allows − 1) ± 0.54 (home / away)

In same-night streamer choices it picked the better player more often in 12 of 13 seasons; worth about ±2–3 points at the extremes.

Ceiling = best game of ROS FP/G + matchup + boost + 1.40 × SD SD² = (n × his SD this season² + 20 × prior²) / (n + 20) prior = his game-to-game SD last season (else 9.06 + 0.145 × FP/G)

Of four methods tested, this was the best calibrated: in 2021–26, 10.6% of games beat it (target 10%).

back-to-backs: rest sit = played the night before AND the team's next game, missed the second night P(sit) = (¼ × sits last season + sits this season + 8 × 0.11) / (¼ × second nights last season + second nights this season + 8) play on a second night = 1 − max(0, P(sit) − 0.076)

Sitting habits carry over between seasons only partly (correlation 0.25), so each rate is pulled toward the league’s 11%; 0.076 is the rest rate on ordinary nights. This season’s second nights count four times as much as last season’s (Nov 2026 test: better at predicting later second nights in 14 of 15 test checkpoints than counting both seasons equally), so a player who has been playing his back-to-backs this year moves quickly. Players on your “won’t play back-to-backs” list get P(sit) = 1.

Injury-risk players who start healthy (tested Nov 2026): players projected for under ~57 games who played 90%+ of their team’s games early went on to play 72% of the rest, on average. The availability blend already expected 80% for them, so trusting the healthy start faster made projections worse (21 of 25 test checkpoints). The risk tends to come back; their availability rises with every game played, at the normal pace.

Start / sit

On My week, for each remaining night of this matchup when more of your players have a game than you have starter slots. Each player’s value that night is the per-game formula above: rest-of-season FP/G, plus any injury boost, scaled by a return ramp, plus the matchup (opponent pace, FP the opponent allows to his position, FG% it allows, home or away), times the chance he plays (back-to-back habit, game-day injury report). Ceiling ranks by a 1-in-10 good night instead (value + 1.40 × his game-to-game SD, the tested Waivers ceiling), which is the better aim when you are behind. The start list is the exact best assignment to your slots, positions respected. For each bench player the page forces him in, re-solves, and shows who would sit and what it costs. The percentage is a rough chance he outscores that starter tonight (a normal approximation with each player’s game-to-game SD, games treated as independent). It is untested as a probability.

Live tab

Fantasy points come straight from ESPN: your league’s feed for that day lists every roster with its lineup slots and each player’s stats and points, and ESPN updates them during games. Clocks and scores come from ESPN’s NBA scoreboard. The page re-reads both every 30 seconds while a game is on; before the first tip-off it reads nothing and waits for the scheduled tip time (then once a minute until ESPN shows the game started), and it pauses when the tab is out of view. Each player’s bar shows his points so far against our projection for the game, on a scale that runs half again past the projection (further if he goes past it), with the estimated rest of his game in a lighter shade. The projected final is the live score plus what is still to come: a starter whose game has not started counts his full projected value for the game (the same per-game value as My week, incl. matchup and back-to-back odds), one in progress counts that value times the share of 48 minutes left (overtime counts as nothing left), a finished game counts nothing; days after today use My week’s best lineup. Today’s lineup is the one actually set on ESPN, not our best one. The win chance is rough: the projected margin against the spread of the games still to come, treated as independent. Untested as a probability.

Streaming planner

ESPN stops a pickup from playing on a day once that day’s first NBA game has tipped off, so the page reads the day’s tip times from ESPN’s live scoreboard and, from the first tip-off on, plans from tomorrow (the Today button is crossed out). Rosters and adds used come live from your ESPN league; press Refresh after you make a move and they are re-read. My week follows the same rule: after the first tip-off, today’s column uses the roster that was locked at tip-off (a player you drop afterwards still counts tonight; nothing after today), and a player you pick up counts from tomorrow on. Both rows are marked (“dropped · today only”, “added · from tomorrow”).

For the rest of the current matchup, each day’s lineup is filled exactly as on My week (the best legal assignment of that day’s players to your starter slots, each game valued with the formula above). You say how many players you’ll drop; by default those are your lowest by rest-of-season points (ROS FP/G × projected games left), keep list excluded, and you can tick others. Each roster spot you give up (or an open one) then gets the best sequence of free agents across the remaining days, found exactly by dynamic programming over day, adds used and who holds the spot, for every number of adds. The adds left this matchup are split across spots by a small knapsack, and the spots are re-solved against each other until the total stops improving. “Most games” counts expected starts (each projected point worth 1/200 of a start, so a whole start always wins but a much better player beats a small rest risk); “Most points” counts projected points. Up to four options are shown: the best plan, the best plan using none of its players (in case they’re taken), the best plan for the other aim, and the best single move. Under each pickup, “Or instead” lists the next-best free agents for exactly the same days, with the rest of the plan unchanged; “rest risk” marks one who may sit a back-to-back night. An add must earn at least 0.3 starts or 2 points. Free agents only: a waiver claim doesn’t clear in time. The planner considers the best 40 free agents over the window plus the best 6 on each single day. Checked: every day’s lineup matches an independent solver, and with one droppable player no schedule beats the plan (brute force over every switch day and pickup).

9. Recap tab

For each game: both box scores (starters and bench as ESPN lists them), each player’s FP under your league, his luck-adjusted FP (section 4) and luck = FP − luck-adjusted. Team luck = Σ luck of players with a draft-day line (two-way call-ups have no luck number).

10. My week tab

each remaining day: value_i = play × (ROS FP/G + matchup + boost) lineup = the assignment of players to slots (PG SG SF PF C G F UTIL×3) that maximises Σ value, each player only in slots ESPN says he is eligible for your ticks: forced start = placed first if a slot fits, untick = benched projected final = live ESPN score + Σ remaining lineups win chance = Φ( (A − B) / √(Σ SD² of every start left) ) adds limit = adds per game day × game days in the matchup, rounded down (e.g. 4/7 × 6 game days = 3)

The week list is your league’s own matchup schedule: ESPN’s map of matchups to weeks when the league was pulled, otherwise the League tab’s playoff weeks and weeks per round. Multi-week playoff rounds appear as one entry (Semifinals, Finals). ESPN stores the add limit per game day, so a week with an off-day gets fewer adds and a longer matchup more; ESPN doesn’t document the rounding, so rounding down is assumed.

The assignment is exact (the Hungarian algorithm), so positions are filled properly, not just ten bodies; each start shows the slot it fills, and the open-slots row names the open positions. The win chance treats games as independent and is untested as a probability.

Start of week is what each team was projected to score over the whole matchup on the morning it began, rebuilt after the fact without hindsight: the team’s roster on the matchup’s first day (read from ESPN for that day), each player’s in-season projection re-run on only the games played before the week, the week’s schedule with the same matchup and back-to-back adjustments, and the same best-lineup assignment. A player on the IR slot that day, or who had missed his team’s last two games, counts as out for the week. Injury boosts, game-day statuses and return ramps are left out (they can’t be rebuilt for a past day), and the matchup factors are today’s. Each team’s header splits the change since then into so far (points scored minus what the start-of-week lineup was expected to score on the days already played) and rest of week (the projection for the remaining days now minus then: injuries, roster moves, lineup choices). Wk vs proj is each player’s points in this week’s games minus his start-of-week projection for those games. The start-of-week number doesn’t know about pickups made during the week, so active streamers tend to beat it. The How accurate is the start-of-week projection? panel on My week scores it on every finished week: typical miss, lean (final minus projection), share within 10%, winners called, and each fantasy team’s average result against its projection (partly a measure of management: streaming beats it, leaving injured players in falls short). After weeks 1–2 (16 team-weeks): typical miss 120 points (8%), 11 of 16 within 10%, lean −55 (it assumes a perfect daily lineup and no mid-week injuries), winner called in 6 of 8.

Extra scoring rules (fouls and milestone games)

Some leagues score technical fouls, flagrant fouls, personal fouls, ejections, fouling out, or bonuses for 40- and 50-point, 15-assist and 20-rebound games. When your league does, they are inside every number on the site (FP/G, totals, Waivers, My week, trades, Lock-In), and the Projections tab adds a column for each one. Box scores supply the actual counts every game. Projections, tested before shipping (written down first; milestone models chosen on 2012–19 and graded once on 2021–26; foul habits tuned on 2025-26 halves and graded on this season so far):

  • Technical fouls are a habit: a player’s own rate (last season plus this one) predicts far better than the league average, so it is used as is (players under 20 games lean on the league average).
  • Flagrant fouls are close to random: mostly the league average (320 games of it blended in). Personal fouls: own rate with 10 games of league average; ejections and ESPN’s foul-out count: own rate.
  • Milestone games: the chance in each game he plays, from his projected average and his own game-to-game swing. 40-point games use a normal curve (the only model within 20% of the real count: it runs 14% low); 50 points, 15 assists and 20 rebounds use the league’s real game shape, which runs 38–53% high on these rare nights (no model was within 20%). A 50-point game also counts as a 40-point game.

Sleeper Lock-In leagues

one game per starter counts each week (Mon–Sun); never locked = his last game, 0 if he sits games left g1 … gK, each: play chance p, projected score v, spread sd after the last game V = p × v each earlier game V = (1 − p) × V + p × E[ max(score, V) ], score ~ Normal(v, sd) lock a finished game when its score ≥ V of the games still to come

For Sleeper leagues using Lock-In, My week, the Lock or pass? card and Waivers use this rule. Each game’s projected score is the Waivers per-game value (matchup and back-to-back included) and its spread is the same one behind the Waivers ceiling. Tested on 2024-25 and 2025-26 (about 2,700 player-weeks each, written down before running): locking this way scored about 5 more points per starter per week than Sleeper’s default (+27%), the simple rule lock any game at or above his average did almost as well, and a starter’s projected weekly value landed within 5% of what he actually banked. Sleeper doesn’t publish which games you locked, so My week assumes the rule (★ marks the game it takes); its live score is Sleeper’s own. Waivers ranks free agents by their expected locked score for each week in the window; the streaming planner is for every-game leagues and steps aside.

11. Trade analyzer

V(roster) = Σ ROS points of its best 13 players, chosen from the roster + free agents (keep list always in, IR slot outside) score = V(after) − V(before) for your side box-score view = Σ actual FP/G × games left (in) − (out) playoff view = same as score with ROS FP/G × playoff games roughly even if |score| < 3% of what you send past trades: then = ROS points on the trade date (the in-season update re-run with only games before that day) now = FP scored since the trade + ROS points from today

Because free agents are in both rosters’ baselines, a player you get only counts for what he adds over the waiver wire, a 2-for-1’s open spot is filled by the best free agent, and nothing is credited for a pickup you could make today anyway.

12. How good is it?

  • Draft rankings: beats last season’s totals in 22 of 24 seasons and last season’s per-game in 20 of 24.
  • Stat by stat against ESPN’s preseason projections (7 seasons, top 200): smaller error on most stats (rebounds, steals, blocks, threes, games); ESPN is better on stars and slightly better on minutes.
  • Game by game against Sleeper’s projections (Rotowire): the Projection scorecard on the Projections tab. Both projections are saved each morning before tip-off and graded on the games played, stat by stat and in fantasy points. It started on Nov 8, 2026; Sleeper’s earlier numbers were edited after the games, so they aren’t graded.
  • The FantasyPros expert consensus is still about 1 point ahead per season on draft value, mostly from earlier injury and role news; ESPN’s published rankings are about even.
  • In-season, the draft-day prior stops mattering after about five games.

13. Data and privacy

Box scores: ESPN’s public fantasy feed and game summaries; history: NBA game logs back to 1996–97; injuries: the official NBA report. The site rebuilds around 6 am Pacific and the injury report hourly on game days; the Refresh button re-reads everything. Your league’s rosters, injuries, waivers and live scores are read straight from ESPN by your browser. Leagues, your team, keep lists, ticks and marks are saved in this browser only.

14. Limits

  • Box scores lag news: a role change shows up after it happens. Use the Injury boosts panel to mark news early.
  • Early in the season small samples swing; small gaps are noise.
  • Matchup and ceiling sizes were measured under this site’s default scoring and are scaled under yours.
  • The win chance is rough; trades and drops compare season points without enforcing positions (My week does).
