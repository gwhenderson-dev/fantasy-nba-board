How it works

Everything on this site comes from one model built on NBA box scores. No outside projections are mixed in: ESPN and FantasyPros rankings appear only as benchmarks to grade the model against. Every piece below was tested on past seasons before it shipped, and the tests that failed are kept in the record too.

1. The draft-day projection

Every stat is projected separately (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, threes, turnovers, makes and attempts), per minute, and then multiplied by projected minutes and games. Because the site ships stat lines rather than fantasy points, your browser can score them under any league’s rules.

FP per game = Σ stat per minute × minutes × your points for that stat + bonuses × how often he earns them season value = FP per game × games
  • Production per minute: a weighted average of the last three seasons, 7 / 2 / 1 (newest heaviest), minutes-weighted. More recency beat the classic 5 / 4 / 3 in 11 of 12 test seasons; longer memories (four or five seasons) and two-season memories both did worse.
  • Age: players improve into their mid-20s and decline after 27; the decline is gentler for players good enough to keep their role (old stars hold up, old role players don’t). Removing the age curve made drafts significantly worse.
  • Minutes and games are projected on their own, so durability is part of the number. Minutes lean 30% toward his minutes over his last 20 games (role changes late last season carry over); games come from his recent availability.
  • Team context: a crowded rotation costs a little production (a small fitted correction).
  • Preseason form: veterans’ preseason production per minute nudges the projection (it cut the error in 11 of 12 test seasons).
  • On-court impact: last season’s plus-minus per 36 minutes predicts next season’s production beyond the box score (it cut the error in 11 of 12 test seasons; raw plus-minus did as well as fancier impact metrics).
  • Injury news at the draft: a player listed out on opening night loses about 30 games for a severe injury (surgery, Achilles, ACL, fracture) or about 12 for anything else, or the team’s announced timeline when there is one. Players returning from a lost season get a production dock that depends on how good they were (stars mostly get their role back; role players often don’t).
  • Rookies come from a college model: college production, draft slot and the minutes open on his new team, blended with his preseason form.

The board is frozen at tip-off so it can be graded honestly at the end of the season; the Projections tab keeps it as the “Preseason (draft day)” view.

2. During the season

After every game the projection is re-blended with what the player is actually doing. The question is how much to trust a few games. It was answered on 24 past seasons (tuned on 1999–2019, then scored once on 2021–26). The draft-day line counts as this much evidence:

production per minute 600 minutes minutes per game 5 games availability 20 games

That blend beat both “never update” and “trust the season so far” at all 25 test checkpoints. This rest-of-season (ROS) FP/G is the projection used everywhere in-season. Projected games left = his availability × his team’s remaining games.

3. Luck

Some of a big game carries into the next one and some doesn’t. Every game’s surprise (actual minus expected) splits exactly into pieces, and each piece was tested for whether it predicts the next game, over 154,305 player-games (2016–26):

  • Carries over (real): extra minutes (about a quarter of a minutes spike persists), extra shot attempts, and extra assists (passing creates assists; whether teammates make the shots doesn’t matter).
  • Doesn’t carry over (luck): shooting percentage (a hot night from three is slightly anti-persistent), steals, blocks, rebounds and free-throw percentage above his own norm. A turnover spike reverses (it comes back the other way).
luck-adjusted = his own rates × minutes played with his real shots, at his own % and his real assists luck = actual FP − luck-adjusted FP

Positive luck means the box score flatters him; negative means he played better than it looks. “His own rates” are the draft-day projection, so a five-steal night from a one-steal player is mostly luck. The luck number is built as a stat line, so it re-scores under your league too.

Tested and rejected: feeding the luck-adjusted numbers into the rest-of-season update. The blend already discounts luck through how slowly it trusts new games; removing luck first made it no better.

4. What each tab does

League

Your league’s rules: scoring, bonuses, roster slots, adds, playoff weeks. Pull them from a public ESPN league or type them in; save several leagues and switch at the top. “You are” picks your team for the in-season tabs.

Projections

The top 300 by total points: rest of season, the frozen draft-day board, or the season so far with luck alongside. The waiver line marks how many players your league rosters (teams × roster spots).

In-season

  • Trade targets: players on other teams whose luck-adjusted FP/G is above their actual FP/G. Their managers see the box score, which is running behind the real level. Early in the season managers judge players against draft cost, so “Now vs draft” shows who is disappointing on paper.
  • Sell high: your players whose FP/G is inflated by luck.
  • Drops: each of your players against the best free agent sharing a position, on rest-of-season points. A swap is only called if it gains more than 8% (smaller is inside projection noise); your keep list and IR slot are never suggested.
  • Rosters: every team ranked by projected rest-of-season points, with how much luck each roster has run.

Waivers

Free agents with a game in the window you pick:

  • Projected = ROS FP/G + a one-game matchup adjustment from four factors tested on 224,300 player-games over 16 seasons: opponent pace, fantasy points the opponent allows to his position, FG% the opponent allows, and home court. In head-to-head streamer choices it picked the better player more often in 12 of 13 seasons. It is worth about ±2–3 points at the extremes, a tiebreaker.
  • Ceiling = a top-10% night: projection + 1.40 × his game-to-game spread, the spread shrunk toward last season’s. Of four methods tested, this was the best calibrated: in 2021–26, 10.6% of games beat it (the target is 10%), and it beat a flat ratio in all 5 test seasons.
  • Back-to-backs: who rests on second nights is read from the game logs, whatever the injury tag said. A second night counts as a rest sit only if he played the night before and his team’s next game. Sitting habits carry over between seasons, but only partly (correlation 0.25), so each player’s rate is pulled toward the league’s 11%. You can add players you know sit.

Recap

Every game of a night with both box scores, starters and bench separated, and each player’s fantasy points, luck-adjusted points and luck; then your players’ night and the biggest hot and cold games.

My week

Every matchup this week and next. Each remaining day’s lineup is the best possible assignment of that day’s players to your starter slots (an exact optimization using ESPN’s position eligibility), valued with the Waivers projection. Projected final = ESPN’s live score + those lineups. Untick anyone you know is sitting. Open starter slots show the days you can stream.

Trade analyzer

The score is your change in rest-of-season points of your best 13, chosen from your roster plus the free agents you could pick up, before and after the trade. A player you get only counts for what he adds over the waiver wire, a 2-for-1’s open spot is filled by the best free agent, taking back more forces your weakest player out, and nothing is credited for a pickup you could make today anyway. The box-score view shows how a manager who goes by averages sees the same deal.

5. How good is it?

Graded on 24 past seasons, each projected only from what was known before that season:

  • It beats simply ranking by last season’s totals in 22 of 24 seasons and by last season’s per-game average in 20 of 24.
  • Stat by stat against ESPN’s own preseason projections (7 seasons, top 200 players), it has the smaller error on most stats (rebounds, steals, blocks, threes, games played among them). ESPN is better on the stars and slightly better on minutes.
  • Human experts are still a little ahead on draft rankings: the FantasyPros consensus captures about 1 point more of a perfect draft’s value per season, mostly by knowing injury and role news earlier. ESPN’s published rankings are about even with the model.
  • In-season, the draft-day prior stops mattering after about five games: from then on the blend is as accurate whichever preseason projection it started from, and it beats a frozen preseason projection by a growing margin.

6. Data and privacy

Box scores come from ESPN’s public fantasy feed and game summaries (scores, starters, did-not-play reasons), with NBA game logs back to 1996–97 for the history the tests run on. The site rebuilds every morning around 6 am Pacific. Your league’s rosters, injuries, waivers and live scores are read straight from ESPN by your browser when you open a tab. Leagues, your team, keep lists and checkboxes are saved in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere. Private ESPN leagues can’t be read.

7. Limits, stated plainly

  • The model reads box scores, not news. A role change, injury or trade shows up after it happens, not before.
  • Early in the season every number rests on a handful of games; small gaps are noise.
  • The matchup adjustment and ceiling spread were measured under this site’s default scoring; under very different scoring they are scaled, so treat them as approximate.
  • The My week win chance is rough: it treats games as independent and has not been tested against real matchup results.
  • The trade analyzer and drops compare rest-of-season points without enforcing positions; in an 8-team league with utility slots that rarely matters, and My week has the exact daily lineup math.
