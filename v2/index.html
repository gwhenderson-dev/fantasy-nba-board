Back-to-back sitters

Detected from the game logs (last season + this one): a second night counts as a rest sit only when the player played the night before and his team’s next game, so injury spells don’t count, whatever the injury tag said. Players who sat a lot of second nights last season kept doing it the next season, just less often (correlation 0.25), so each rate is pulled toward the league’s 11%. How it works

Everything here comes from one model built on NBA box scores — no outside projections are mixed in. ESPN and FantasyPros rankings appear only as benchmarks.

The preseason projection

Each player’s per-minute production is a weighted average of his last three seasons (7 / 2 / 1, newest heaviest), adjusted for age, for how crowded his team’s rotation is, and for his preseason form. Minutes and games played are projected separately, so durability is part of the number. Every stat is projected on its own (points, rebounds, steals…), which is why any scoring system can be applied in your browser.

During the season

The projection updates game by game: the preseason line is blended with what the player is actually doing, with the amount of trust tested on 24 past seasons (production after about 600 minutes, minutes after about five games, availability after about twenty).

Luck

Some of a hot game persists and some doesn’t. Tested over 154,305 player-games: extra minutes, extra shot attempts and extra assists carry into the next game; shooting percentage, steals, blocks and rebounds above a player’s own norm do not. So each game splits into:

luck-adjusted FP = his own per-minute rates × the minutes he played + his real shot attempts at his own shooting % + his real assists luck = actual FP − luck-adjusted FP

A positive luck number means the box score flatters him; negative means he played better than it looks.
